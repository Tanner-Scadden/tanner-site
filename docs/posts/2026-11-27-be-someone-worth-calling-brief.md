# Brief: Be Someone Worth Calling

**Title:** Be Someone Worth Calling (working title was References I Never
Asked For)

**Summary:** Every big turn in my life came from someone vouching for me
without my asking: a spot on Sentinels, my first contracting client, the calls
I still get today. None of it was strategy. It came from being myself: kind,
useful, and giving my best.

**Slug:** `references-i-never-asked-for`

## Reader

Early to mid-career people, probably technical but not only. They've heard "be
useful", "high agency" and "build your network" as slogans, and have been
told to network, build a personal brand, and ask for referrals. They haven't
seen what the alternative looks like across a real career. Esports terms (ADC,
org, worlds) need a few words of context. *(Reader is my call.)*

## Takeaway

You don't collect references. They come from being yourself, consistently:
being kind, being useful, and giving your best when nobody is keeping score.
People remember how you showed up, and they vouch for you in rooms you're not in.
As your career grows, that network grows on its own.

## Angle

**References I Never Asked For** (the wildcard, #20). The theme is that
references come from being yourself, not from asking, networking or keeping
score. The calls are the evidence: each section is one time someone vouched for
Tanner unprompted, and the backstory of what they'd seen.

- **Criteria:** Theirs (built on Tanner's own stories), Reader fit, Takeaway,
  Fresh. Scored ✅ on all four.
- **Shortlist:** The Calls; Anatomy of One Phone Call; The Bar You Set for the
  Room; wildcard: References I Never Asked For.
- **Why this one:** Tanner picked it ("a really beautiful foundation"), with the
  theme that references come from being yourself. It absorbs The Calls (same
  stories) and gives it a sharper thesis. It also quietly argues with the
  favor-bank framing, including the joke version in Sunil's title: you don't
  bank them, you just are that person.
- **Gimmick risk** (why it was the wildcard): handled by treating "references"
  as the theme, not a rigid format. Sections are stories, not mock reference
  letters.

## Hook

Open on a teammate calling. Tanner had stopped believing he'd play again after the
Immortals retainer. He asks if he wants to play, because Sentinels needs an
ADC. Then the turn: that teammate was vouching for him, and Tanner never asked. The first
sentence should land the moment, with no setup paragraph
(*Smart Brevity*'s point, applied to the post itself).

## Outline

1. **The call** — A teammate asks if he'd play again; Sentinels needs an ADC. A
   childhood dream of playing League, for an org that big. Name it as a
   reference he never asked for.
2. **What that teammate had seen** — Back up to Arena of Valor. Wanting to be the best,
   making friends in the game, being kind to people. The org invite, and
   Immortals paying a retainer before the game came out. Point: he wasn't
   trying to earn anything. That's just how he played.
3. **The reference nobody could give** — The counterexample. Worlds, with subs:
   the synergy built all season wasn't there, and the team got disgruntled.
   Nobody could vouch for chemistry that didn't exist. Same pattern on
   engineering teams: a wrong hire changes the culture and people work around
   them. *(Framing is mine; the story is Tanner's.)*
4. **The night I went independent** — The leap of faith that the world would
   figure it out. That night, an engineer he'd worked with called with the
   Webaholics gig. Then more calls, and years of contracting purely from word
   of mouth from people who'd worked with him.
5. **What being yourself looked like** — Short and concrete, not a list of
   rules. Building really cool things wherever he worked and pushing his
   knowledge. Finding the problems instead of having them spoon-fed, and
   bringing the team solutions. Being intentional with the first sentence.
   Jumping in when someone needs help; when he doesn't know how, telling them
   they're not alone and he's one call or text away. Believing in himself:
   every ceiling except physics is one you put there. Surrounding himself with
   people with the same level of ambition. Collaboration over being right: the
   goal is the team getting there, not winning the argument. Mack's evil twin
   fits here, briefly, as a tool.
6. **They're still calling** — He still gets calls asking if he's open to other
   work, or to founding a company. The graph grows with the career. Close on
   the references: Sunil's fundraiser that "took fifteen years to raise", and
   Arnold's dad telling him "Be useful, Arnold." Then Tanner's favorite quote,
   from *The Lorax*: "Unless someone like you cares a whole awful lot, nothing
   is going to get better. It's not." End on Tanner's own line: be yourself,
   and let people vouch for that.

## Tanner's material

- "I built my career around trying to build really cool things everywhere I've
  worked, and pushing my knowledge however I could."
- Esports: got really into the games and wanted to be the best; made friends in
  the game; was eventually invited to join an esports org.
- "Being invited to join Sentinels was purely because of my kindness in Arena
  of Valor."
- Immortals paid him to be on retainer before the game came out. After the
  retainer he "didn't really believe in playing again", until a teammate
  called asking if he'd want to play again; they needed an ADC.
- "My childhood dream of playing League, and for an org as well known as
  Sentinels, all because I always want to be useful and kind."
- Worlds: subs came with the team; "the synergy that we had built up that
  season wasn't there at those moments and team became disgruntled, and I've
  seen this pattern repeat as an engineer. The wrong hires change the team
  culture / make people work around them."
- Webaholics: "an engineer I worked with giving me a call when he heard I was
  starting my own thing. I took a big leap of faith that the world would figure
  it out, and funny enough it happened that night." Multiple other people called
  too; numerous jobs "purely from word of mouth of friends that had worked with
  me in the past."
- "Today if I know anyone needs help, I immediately jump in to help how I can.
  If I am not sure how, I reassure them that they're not alone, and I'm one call
  or text away."
- "Find the problems, don't have them be spoon fed to you. Solve them / come to
  the team with the solutions. Be intentional with how you communicate."
- "You get to live once, don't squander it by thinking you can't do something.
  Everything (except physics) is an imaginary ceiling that you are putting on
  yourself."
- "Just as important to surround yourself with people who have high standards."
- "I still get calls from people I've worked with/for asking if I am open to
  other work / founding a company with them. As my career grows, the network
  graph does naturally as well."
- "The references come from being yourself."
- The ideas to sprinkle through, in his words: "Have high agency, chase your
  dreams, surround yourself with people who have the same level of ambition,
  help others, and be kind, sprinkled with opportunities that have come my way
  because of this."
- "The importance of collaboration, not being right."
- His favorite quote, *The Lorax* (Dr. Seuss): "Unless someone like you cares a
  whole awful lot, nothing is going to get better. It's not."
- Site facts to draw on: Allegiance, Sentinels, 2× NA champion, MVP, 3 trips to
  worlds; Wild Rift Icons 2022 in Singapore with Sentinels; Webaholics 2021–22,
  tech lead of six.

### References (support, never the subject)

- Arnold Schwarzenegger, *Be Useful*: title from his father's line "Be useful,
  Arnold"; "Never Think Small" (ceilings); "Break Your Mirrors" (no one is

- Sunil Pai, ["collecting favours"](https://sunilpai.dev/posts/collecting-favours):
  his dad the town doctor; the fundraiser that "took fifteen years to raise";
  "just help them with the problem first. figure out the rest later." Note that
  Sunil also keeps no tally; the "collecting" is a joke. Agree with him, don't
  argue.
- *Smart Brevity* (VandeHei, Allen, Schwartz): the first sentence decides
  whether you're read at all.

## Anti-goals and out of scope

- Not a book report. Each reference gets a sentence or two, max.
- Not a listicle of habits. Section 5 stays short and grounded in his examples.
- Not mock reference letters. The theme carries it, not the format.
- Not a LinkedIn post: no humblebrag tone, no "here's what I learned" bullets.
- Not a networking how-to or a hustle sermon.
- **Out of scope:** church and service growing up (dropped, could be its own
  post); a full post on hiring and team culture ("What Broke at Worlds").

## Length and tone

1,500 to 2,000 words. Warm, plain, specific, first person. Matches the front
page: short sentences, real names and numbers, no em dashes (site rule).
